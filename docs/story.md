# The story of Chronoheart

**Decided with Logan, 2026-10-20.** This replaces every earlier version of the story (Orin as the hour thief, borrowed hours, the Tide Clock, the First Clockmaker loop, the three age endings). Older canon in [IDEAS.md](../IDEAS.md) is kept only as history. Write all dialogue from this page, in the voice described at the end.

---

## In one paragraph

Fifty years ago, on Midwinter Night at the Highcrest observatory, Lyra was dying. She had built the Chronoheart, and she asked her two dearest friends, Donald Eugene and Orin, not to use it to save her. Orin let her go. Don took it and ran. He has been rewriting history ever since: thousands of attempts to save her, every one of them a failure, because death is the one thing that cannot be prevented. And on the way, the mender found he liked mending the whole world to suit himself. He grew rich and powerful through changes nobody else can remember, until he came to believe he was the author of reality, an emperor ruling from the End of Time. Vivienne is his latest attempt: a brilliant mind he arranged to bring to his door, and then set loose, without telling her, against the locks he built to protect his version of the world. What he never knew is that the Chronoheart lets whoever holds it remember every change. So while Vivienne mends the valley, she sees him rewriting it too.

---

## The rules

1. **The Chronoheart is the only thing in the world that touches time.** It lets its holder step between a place's ages (its Past, Present and Future). Every other power is a part fitted to it: the Seal ring, the Echo lens, the Tempo escape wheel, Rewind, the Loupe, the Lantern. No clock, bell, watch or well is magic.
2. **Whoever holds a Chronoheart remembers every change.** Everyone else lives in the new version and never knows there was an old one. Hobb has always had his millpond. Saltwick has always been dry.
3. **Lyra built it in two halves.** The amulet, and a second half. Whoever holds one half remembers every change the other half makes. She meant it so that no one could ever rewrite the world alone. Don never knew this. He thought remembering was his own gift: proof that he stood outside the story.
4. **Death cannot be prevented.** Don has stopped floods, fires and falls, and for everyone else they never happened. But a person whose time has come finds another way to go. Lyra has died 8,511 different ways.

---

## The people

**Donald Eugene.** The clockmaker of Hollowmere, and the antagonist. Charming, funny, generous on the surface, which is why nobody suspects him. Underneath: cold, impatient, and completely certain he is right. He began by trying to save Lyra, and he still is; that is the human root of him. But fifty years of rewriting have made people into pieces he arranges, and the world into a draft he corrects. He does not love Vivienne. She is Attempt 8512. His warmth toward her is how he steers her.

**Vivienne.** His apprentice, and the player. Clever, practical, a little reckless. She believes in her master completely at the start. Because she holds the other half of the Chronoheart, she is the first person in fifty years to see what he does. She already knows, in her bones, the thing he never learned: that things are allowed to end, and that the ending is what makes them matter. She never says it. She acts on it.

**Lyra.** Built the Chronoheart and the parts that fit it (she ground the Echo lens's glass). Died fifty years ago on Midwinter Night, gently, of an illness. Before she died she used the Chronoheart's echo to send echoes of herself down the years, to whoever Don would one day give the other half to. In every chapter she is only the **Mysterious Stranger**: no name, cryptic, kind. Through the time lens she flickers blue, like an echo. She is recognized only at the end.

**Orin.** An ordinary man, with no Chronoheart and no memory of other versions. Lyra's other dearest friend. He let her go, and he has stayed on the mountain ever since, keeping the observatory and her cup. Prickly, proud, grieving honestly. He has not seen Don in fifty years and has no idea what Don has done.

**Dr. Pell.** Saltwick's old doctor. Fifty years ago she meant to sail away for good, and never could. She is the clearest picture of what Don's control costs one ordinary person, and she will never know he did it.

**Zach and Megan.** Squirrels. Comedy, every chapter. Never over the saddest moments.

## Don's empire

He did not set out to rule anything. But every change he made could be made a little in his favor, and over fifty years they added up.

- **His mark.** Don stamps every clock and machine he makes with a small maker's mark: a gold cog with a notch cut in one tooth. Vivienne has seen it on his workbench her whole life. In his versions of the world, that mark is everywhere, and nobody knows whose it is.
- **The Present** shows the empire quietly: a patron whose name nobody knows; the Gilded Gear Company, which owns more and more of each town; in Saltwick, the only working well, where water is sold by the cup.
- **The Futures are where his versions are heading.** Each chapter's Future is the world Don is steering toward, and his mark is on all of it:
  - Hollowmere's **Age of Chrome**: the towers, the drones, the security all carry the gold cog.
  - Saltwick's **Dry Age**: salt flats, and the water company's pumps.
  - Highcrest's **Stopped Age**: the whole world sealed in its moment, perfect and still, kept exactly as he wants it.
- **The End of Time** is his seat: a throne room of black marble beyond the last age, with every crack mended in gold (he mends everything). From there he sees every age at once, like pages, and rewrites them.
- **The challenges in every chapter are his defenses.** The locks, gates, Wardens and frost exist to protect his version of the world. Sending Vivienne through them is also the test: if she can get past his own locks, she can solve anything.
- **The Wardens are his machines,** one guarding each change he cares about. Wherever there is a Warden, Don changed something.
- **The Lost Cogs are his,** stamped with his mark: parts of his machines and his defenses. Collecting them is collecting evidence. The Cog Finder finds his work.

When Don is stopped, the Futures stop heading where he steered them. (This is already how the post-game Futures work: Saltwick's Dry Age becomes the Age of Lights.)

## Before the game

1. Fifty years ago: Lyra falls ill on Highcrest. She asks Don and Orin not to use the Chronoheart for her. Orin agrees. Don takes it and runs.
2. Attempt after attempt: earlier doctors, other winters, other towns. Each one fails, and changes the valley. He fixes the side effects, and the fixes of the fixes.
3. **Saltwick.** One early attempt was Dr. Pell, the best healer on the coast, who meant to sail away that autumn. Don went far into the Past, to the age of the tide folk, and turned the river that feeds Saltwick's harbor down another valley with a small, clever sluice. Over four hundred years the river slowly found its new course, and the last of the water left Saltwick the very autumn Dr. Pell meant to sail. Her ship sat in the mud. She stayed. That winter she was called up the mountain to Lyra, and Lyra died anyway. Saltwick has been in drought for fifty years, and the Gilded Gear Company sells it water.
4. Somewhere along the way, he stopped only trying to save Lyra, and started arranging everything else as well. The empire grew.
5. **Attempt 8512.** Out of ideas, he looks for the cleverest mind he can find, and arranges for her to end up at his door: a clock in her village stops, and her family sends her to the nearest clockmaker, in Hollowmere. He takes her on and teaches her, and waits until she is ready.

## Chapter One: The Clocktower of Hollowmere (the exam)

**What happens.** Don stops the Tower (it is only a great clock; he broke it so she would have something to fix) and vanishes. He leaves the amulet half of the Chronoheart on the workbench. Vivienne knows he would never leave it behind, so she believes he is in real trouble, which is exactly why he left it. His satchels along her road are the course material; his notes are the exam paper. He watches from other ages and adjusts the exam as she goes.

**Why the Chronoheart runs down in Chapter One.** He gave it to her limited. The Everspring is the exam's reward for getting far enough.

**What she notices** (she sees his changes as well as her own):
- Her own changes, first, and nobody else's memory of them: Hobb has always had his millpond. Charming, and a bit lonely.
- Then changes she did not make. A shop sign is different. Nobody notices. She shrugs it off.
- **When you are stuck a long time,** a ladder or plank appears that was not there before. "That wasn't there a minute ago." (A hint system, and the first sign that someone is watching and arranging things.)
- A grumpy townsperson is suddenly kind, and does not remember ever being otherwise.
- **The hidden room** (Rampart Row): his notes are addressed to her by name, on paper far older than she is.
- **Oren the Mason** (the Past) remembers an old man in a fine coat who drew the Tower's plans and paid in gold. The coins carry a gold cog with a notched tooth.
- **The Age of Chrome** is covered in that mark.
- **The Warden's Courtyard** has a Warden guarding the Escapement Key: his machine, guarding his lock.

**The ending.** Don "comes back," with a story about being lost in time. He is so proud of her. Then the slip:

> "The oak tree came up really well. What a great idea!"

She never told him about the acorn.

---

## Chapter Two: Saltwick (the second exam)

**What happens.** Don sends her to Saltwick with one question: "Saltwick's been dry for fifty years. Find out why." He knows exactly why. The test is whether she can trace an effect back to a cause hundreds of years earlier, which is practice for Lyra.

**What she finds.**
- A town in drought: a mud harbor, boats aground, empty wells, salt in the fields, and the Gilded Gear Company selling water by the cup.
- **Dr. Pell,** very old, who meant to leave and never did, and remembers a thin woman on the mountain she could not save.
- **The harbor's records** end on one date: Midwinter Night, fifty years ago.
- **His edits are bigger now:** a statue changes; a boat has a new name; the lighthouse stands somewhere slightly different. Once, through the lens, she glimpses a familiar coat in the Past.
- **Maren** (the Past), an elder of the tide folk, saw a stranger in a fine coat build something at the head of the river. "He said the river would be happier going the other way. He was very kind about it."
- **The Harbor Warden** guards the sluice: his machine.
- **The sluice** itself, in the Past: far too well made for its age, with one of his stamped cogs inside it.

**The resolution.** She opens the sluice in the Past. A time-lapse shows four hundred years of river finding its old way home, and the water comes back. The escape at the end of the chapter is the river returning after fifty years, as a flood surge.

**The mirror** (felt, never said): in Chapter One she turned a spring to give Hobb his millpond. Here she finds Don did the same thing to take a whole town's water away.

**The turning point.** Back home, she mentions an edit in passing, almost a joke: "Wasn't the bakery called Fenwick's?" Don goes completely still. In that moment he learns she remembers. From then on it is a quiet duel: he changes small things on purpose and watches her face.

## Chapter Three: Highcrest (the truth)

**Why she climbs.** Don asks her to come up Highcrest for Midwinter: "There's someone I'd like you to meet." He goes on ahead. She climbs.

**The frost** is Don's Stasis Seals: he has held parts of the mountain in their moment so no one else can change them. That is why you cannot shift where it lies.

**The Lantern** is Ingrid's gran's lamp, which Lyra once fitted with her glass. It holds light from another hour. (Tobin the Stargazer has already told you that starlight is old light: light already travels through time.)

**What she learns on the way up.**
- **Orin's plain story.** Lyra was ill; she asked them both not to save her; Don took the Chronoheart and ran. Orin has not seen him since. (Orin is gruff, not a villain. At the first meeting he will not talk to a stranger about it.)
- **The Mysterious Stranger,** a little more pointed each time.
- **The memory under the orrery:** Midwinter Night, fifty years ago. Lyra, Orin, young Don. Lyra asks Don not to use it for her. He promises. This is where the theme is said, once, in Lyra's words. No one else ever says it.
- **People who have lost someone and live with it well:** Ingrid and her gran's lantern; Orin and Lyra's cup.
- **Don's notebooks,** in the observatory: fifty years of attempts, written in his calm craftsman's hand. "Attempt 12: a doctor from Saltwick, brought early. Did not help. The harbor changed." The last line: "Attempt 8512: an apprentice. Arranged. Testing."

She reaches the summit knowing.

### The finale: the End of Time

1. **The shimmer.** At the summit, a shimmer hangs by the Needle. She steps through into the End of Time: his throne room.
2. **The ask.** Don stops pretending. Calmly, proudly, without shame, he tells her everything, and asks: "You're the cleverest attempt I've had. Solve it."
3. **She refuses, at once.** She already suspected him. She has read the notebooks. Lyra was allowed to end.
4. **He tries to force her.** He cannot write her choice, so he rewrites everything else to corner her: "Do it, or I will keep rewriting until you will." The valley changes in the pages around them. Orin is gone. Hollowmere's Tower stops again.
5. **The fight** is surviving him as he unmakes the things she cares about. Her counter is the Stasis Seal, fired from her wrench: it holds people and places in their moment, so his rewrites cannot touch them. One beat of comedy in the middle: she seals Zach and Megan, mid-argument, to keep them safe, and they stand in crystal with a placard.
6. **The last seal is Don himself,** on his throne. The man who never let a moment pass is held still while time goes on around him. For once he cannot change anything. He can only listen.
7. **Lyra's last echo** speaks to him: "It was my ending, Don. Not yours to write. I built it in two, so you'd never be alone with it." He cannot argue, rewrite, or leave. He has to hear it.
8. When the seal fades, his half of the Chronoheart is on the floor. He does not reach for it. **Vivienne picks it up.** Now she holds both halves.

### The ending

- **Don** is an ordinary old man in a world he can no longer change. He remembers every version, and no one will ever believe him. He stays on the mountain with Orin. At Midwinter he sits by Lyra's cup, and for the first time in fifty years he does not reach for anything. The game does not say whether he is sorry.
- **The Futures** stop heading where he steered them.
- **Vivienne** goes home. The workshop in Hollowmere is hers now, and the Tower is ticking. She walks past the bakery. It is still not called Fenwick's. She could change it back with one step into the Past, and she does not. She is the one person who knows how the world was before Don, and she leaves it be.
- **After the credits,** she keeps the whole Chronoheart: the keeper who remembers, and does not rewrite. You can still travel, for the Lost Cogs and the Starlit Garden.

There is one ending.

## How it maps onto the game we have

**Kept:** every level, power and puzzle; the Stranger and the lens clue; Lyra's cup and the memory under the orrery; Orin; the End of Time's throne room (now Don's); the Seal, which becomes the key to the ending.

**Re-dressed:**
- **Chapter One.** The Tower is only a clock Don broke. The "ages coming loose" lore goes. Oren's First Clockmaker drawings become the old man in the fine coat.
- **Chapter Two.** The Tide Clock, the Tide Watch and the tide go. The Tide Well becomes **Don's sluice** at the head of the river; the three bells become the three locks he built on it (a clockmaker's lock); the Harbor Warden guards it; Rewind is a part fitted to the Chronoheart, given by the Stranger. The empty cradle at the bottom becomes the sluice's valve. The tide coming in becomes the river coming back.
- **Chapter Three.** Orin goes from villain to grieving friend. The frost is Don's seals. The Lantern is fitted with Lyra's glass. The fight at the End of Time is Don's.

**Cut:** the three age endings; borrowed hours; the clocks lending each other time; the First Clockmaker loop; the Master Drawings; Vivienne as the First Clockmaker; Michelle the Wise; the Wardens built by a First Clockmaker; the Master's Key undoing a cost.

## Voice

From [the 2026-10-19 story pass](../IDEAS.md), still true:

- **Say the theme once.** Lyra says it, in the memory. Everyone else acts it out. Vivienne never makes a speech about it.
- **Withhold, then show.** Don's crime is learned through things the player sees change, not through anyone explaining it. His notebooks are the confession, and they are written like a craftsman's log.
- **Small, specific things over speeches:** the cup, the bakery sign, the oak, the notched gold cog.
- **Let quiet beats stay quiet.** Comedy is welcome, but never over the saddest moments.
- **Don is never cartoonish.** He is reasonable, warm and sure of himself, right to the end. That is what makes him frightening.
